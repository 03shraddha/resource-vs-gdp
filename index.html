How to read this ▾
What is T=0?

T=0 is the year a country started significant resource extraction — when oil wells or mines went from experimental to actually producing real revenue. Every line in the chart is indexed to 100 at that year, so you're comparing trajectories, not absolute wealth. A country sitting at 200 at T+20 doubled its income per person in 20 years; one at 80 got poorer.

Reading the chart

The x-axis is years since extraction started, not calendar years — so Norway (1971) and Nigeria (1958) can be compared side by side. The shaded band around T=0 is the ±5-year transition window. Dashed lines are pre-1990 estimates spliced from the Maddison Project; treat them as directionally correct but not precise. The dotted vertical line moves with the slider to show your chosen endpoint.

Controls

Slider (bottom-left of chart) moves the "After" endpoint from T+10 to T+60 — drag it to see how rankings shift over time. T−5 / T+5 buttons shift the extraction start date ±5 years to check if findings are sensitive to that choice. Contrast isolates the sharpest divergence pairs. Click a legend dot to hide/show a country; double-click to isolate it solo. Click any table row to read the country's story.

The numbers

Before / After are average annual growth rates in the 10 years before and N years after T=0. Index is just GDP/person at the slider year relative to T=0 — no fancy math, just a ratio. The ⚠ flag means GDP per capita is likely a poor welfare proxy: oil revenues were so concentrated at the top that the average tells you almost nothing about how ordinary people lived.

Data sources

Post-1990 GDP/pc: World Bank WDI indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD (constant 2021 PPP $). Pre-1990 backfill: Maddison Project Database 2023 growth rates spliced to the WB anchor. T=0 dates: manually set to the first year of commercially significant extraction, not discovery. 28 countries across oil, natural gas, diamonds, copper, and phosphate.

Caveats worth knowing

Some T=0 dates (Indonesia 1885, Malaysia 1910) rely heavily on colonial-era Maddison estimates — treat the dashed lines as rough. Nauru and early DRC data are sparse enough that the exact shape is uncertain. The T=0 sensitivity buttons let you stress-test the single biggest assumption: whether extraction start dates are right.
